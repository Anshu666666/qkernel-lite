2. Abstract
PROJECT ABSTRACT & EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
For IEG313: Quantum Computing for Engineers at the Indian Institute of Information Technology (IIIT) Kottayam, our team developed QKernel-Lite, an empirical benchmarking platform evaluating Projected Quantum Kernel Support Vector Machines (PQK-SVM) on pediatric chest radiographs (PneumoniaMNIST) under extreme clinical data scarcity ($N \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$). In scarce clinical regimes, deep convolutional networks fail due to severe parameter under-determination ($P \approx 10^5 \gg N$). While recent literature hypothesizes quantum advantage for small datasets, we conduct a fully paired 960-run empirical study ($n = 15$ seeds, $\text{df} = 14$) with strict 12-test Bonferroni family-wise error control ($\alpha = 0.00417$).

Our findings establish that standard angle encoding ($s = 1.00$) fails due to single-qubit phase wrapping turnaround ($U(\pi)^2|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ up to global phase). While CV-tuned scaling ($s \le 0.25$) suppresses wrapping and recovers competitive performance ($0.7717$–$0.8962$ AUC), M3-tuned remains statistically detectably below classical RBF-SVM (M2) across all sample sizes (deficits of $-0.1241$, $-0.0846$, $-0.0296$, and $-0.0288$ AUC; all survive Bonferroni). The only circuit family statistically equivalent to M2 within $\pm 0.02$ AUC ($N \ge 20$) is the 1-layer product state, which is a per-feature trigonometric map and trivially classically simulable. Multi-qubit entanglement systematically degrades performance, and adding nearest-neighbor $ZZ$ correlators does not reverse this degradation.

4. Introduction
4.1. Background & Problem Statement: Clinical Radiograph Scarcity

In pediatric pulmonology and emergent rare-pathology diagnostics, clinical imaging datasets frequently contain fewer than 100 expert-verified radiographs. Training deep convolutional networks from scratch under such severe sample scarcity ($N \le 100$) leads to catastrophic empirical failure. When model capacity ($P \approx 10^5$ to $10^7$ trainable weights) vastly exceeds training instances ($P \gg N$), empirical risk minimization memorizes spurious background artifacts (e.g., patient orientation tags, scanner contrast steps, rib cage edge shadows) rather than authentic radiological opacities. At $N = 10$, scratch CNNs overfit noise, yielding high variance and poor diagnostic generalizability.

4.2. Motivation & The Quantum Kernel Inductive Bias Hypothesis

Recent quantum machine learning literature hypothesizes that encoding classical data into high-dimensional quantum Hilbert spaces can construct expressive non-linear representations. Specifically, Projected Quantum Kernels (PQK) (Huang et al., 2021; Alagiyawanna et al., arXiv:2601.03375) map classical coordinates into parameterized quantum states $\rho(\mathbf{x})$, then project the state back to classical metric spaces via expectation values of local Pauli observables. This projection was proposed to avoid exponential concentration (barren plateaus) that destroys global fidelity kernels, offering a potential inductive advantage in scarce regimes.

4.3. Objectives & The Three Non-Negotiable Rules of the Scientific Control

A pervasive flaw in published literature is comparing quantum models against unregularized scratch CNNs, claiming "quantum advantage" over an easily beaten strawman baseline. To establish genuine scientific validity, a quantum kernel benchmark must satisfy three strict controls:

  1. Identical Feature Representations: The classical control (Model M2) and quantum model (Model M3) must consume the exact same preprocessed feature coordinates extracted from frozen ResNet-18 and compressed via PCA.
  2. Identical Downstream Optimizer: Both models must use the identical convex dual quadratic program ($C$-SVM) and the identical Gaussian RBF kernel formulation.
  3. Strict Seed & Budget Parity: All models must be evaluated across identical random training subsets with stratified class splits, with any post-hoc search degrees of freedom transparently accounted for.
The Scientific Control Guarantee: Because M2 and M3 share the exact same Gaussian formula $k(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \exp(-\gamma \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|^2)$ and the identical convex dual SVM solver, any observed performance delta isolates the non-linear quantum coordinate transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ as the sole independent variable.
4.4. Scope of the Study

The investigation evaluates binary pediatric pneumonia classification on PneumoniaMNIST across training subset sizes $N \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$ using 15 independent random seeds ($n = 15$, 960 total runs), with a fixed holdout clinical test set ($N_{\text{test}} = 624$).

5. Literature Review
5.1. Related Work: Fidelity Kernels vs. Exponential Concentration

Conventional quantum kernels compute global transition fidelity in Hilbert space:

$$k_{\text{fidelity}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = |\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}) | \psi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle|^2 = \text{Tr}\left(\rho(\mathbf{x})\rho(\mathbf{x}')\right)$$
(1)

As proved by Huang et al. (2021), fidelity kernels suffer from exponential concentration in $n$-qubit systems: as $n$ grows, $\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}) | \psi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle \to 0$ for almost all orthogonal states. The resulting Gram matrix rapidly approaches the identity matrix $K \approx \mathbf{I}$, destroying margin separability. In clinical practice, every patient scan appears completely orthogonal to every other scan, leaving the SVM classifier unable to discover generalizable decision boundaries.

5.2. Existing Methods: Projected Quantum Kernel Formulation

To resolve concentration, Huang et al. proposed projecting the full $n$-qubit state $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ onto reduced 1-particle density matrices, evaluating expectations of local Pauli operators $P_k \in \{\sigma_x^{(i)}, \sigma_y^{(i)}, \sigma_z^{(i)}\}$:

$$E_k(\mathbf{x}) = \text{Tr}\left(\rho(\mathbf{x}) P_k\right), \quad k \in \{1, \dots, 3n\}$$
(2)

The projected quantum kernel evaluates a classical Gaussian RBF over these physical expectation vectors:

$$k_{\text{PQK}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \exp\left(-\gamma \sum_{k=1}^{3n} \left(E_k(\mathbf{x}) - E_k(\mathbf{x}')\right)^2\right)$$
(3)
5.3. Limitations of Prior Studies & The Gap Addressed

Alagiyawanna et al. (SLAAI-ICAI 2024; arXiv:2601.03375) applied projected quantum kernels to small-dataset classification, reporting 95% on MNIST and 90% on CIFAR-10. However, their study evaluated downscaled synthetic toy digits against an unregularized 12% scratch CNN baseline without matched transfer learning controls. QKernel-Lite directly bridges this gap by evaluating genuine medical imaging data under strict matched transfer learning controls.

6.2. What We Did Differently: Key Innovations & Methodological Improvements
Summary of Novel Contributions in QKernel-Lite 1. Authentic Medical Benchmark vs. Synthetic Digits: Replaced downscaled toy classification (MNIST/CIFAR) with clinical pediatric chest radiographs (PneumoniaMNIST), addressing genuine medical opacity detection where data scarcity is clinically motivated.
2. Matched Transfer Learning Baseline (Model M2): Integrated frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 embeddings paired with classical RBF-SVM as the true benchmark control, isolating the quantum feature map as the sole variable.
3. Discovery of the Phase Wrapping Mechanism: Pinpointed the physical turnaround mechanism ($U(\pi)^2|0\rangle = |0\rangle$) causing standard angle encoding ($s = 1.00$) to collapse, and designed post-hoc scale compression ($s \le 0.25$) to restore monotonicity.
4. Bonferroni Family-Wise Error Control: Conducted 960 paired runs across 15 seeds ($\text{df} = 14$) with strict 12-test Bonferroni significance thresholds ($\alpha = 0.00417$).
5. Falsification of the ZZ Correlator Hypothesis: Formulated and empirically disproved the hypothesis that unmeasured 2-qubit correlators caused PQK underperformance by evaluating nearest-neighbor $\langle Z_i Z_{i+1} \rangle$ pairs.

7.2. Mathematical Concepts, Quantum Circuit & State Formulation

Stage 1 (Feature Extraction): 28×28 radiographs are passed through a frozen ResNet-18 backbone. Global average pooling yields a 512-dimensional embedding capturing optical textures without parameter overfitting.
Stage 2 (Dimensionality Reduction): PCA fitted strictly on training folds (preventing data leakage) compresses 512 features to $n = 8$ components (~75% variance), scaled to rotation angles $\theta_i \in [0, \pi]$.
Stage 3 (Quantum Encoding & Measurement): Implemented in PennyLane (lightning.qubit). For $L$ layers, each qubit undergoes single-qubit rotations $R_Z(s\theta_i) R_Y(s\theta_i)$ interleaved with circular CNOT entangling gates. 24 local Pauli expectation values $\langle X_i \rangle, \langle Y_i \rangle, \langle Z_i \rangle$ are extracted to construct $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$.

Small-Scale Classical Duality: For a single qubit in $|0\rangle$, applying $R_Y(s\theta)R_Z(s\theta)$ yields $|\psi\rangle = \cos(s\theta/2)|0\rangle + e^{is\theta}\sin(s\theta/2)|1\rangle$. As $s \to 0$, Taylor series gives $\langle X \rangle \approx s\theta$, $\langle Y \rangle \approx (s\theta)^2$, and $\langle Z \rangle \approx 1 - (s\theta)^2/2$. Small-$s$ quantum kernels match classical accuracy precisely because they behave as smooth classical polynomial approximations.

8. Implementation
8.1. Software Stack, Libraries & Hardware Infrastructure

The implementation utilizes an optimized, deterministic Python scientific computing stack:

  • PennyLane 0.38: High-performance quantum state-vector simulation engine utilizing the C++ accelerated lightning.qubit backend for deterministic expectation value evaluation.
  • PyTorch 2.4: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 feature extraction backbone with frozen weights (11.7 million weights held constant).
  • scikit-learn 1.5: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and convex dual quadratic programming solver (SVC with kernel="precomputed").
  • Streamlit 1.38: Interactive editorial decision-support prototype platform (app.py).
  • Hardware Environment: Executed across AMD/Intel multi-core workstations; average quantum simulation time was 0.082s per 8-qubit circuit forward pass.
8.2. Medical Imaging Dataset: MedMNIST v2 PneumoniaMNIST

The benchmark utilizes PneumoniaMNIST (Yang et al., Scientific Data 2023), comprising 5,856 pediatric chest X-rays (patients aged 1 to 5 years old). The task is binary classification: healthy lung parenchyma versus bacterial/viral pneumonia consolidation. A fixed hold-out clinical test set ($N_{\text{test}} = 624$, 234 normal, 390 pneumonia) was maintained across all experiments. To simulate data scarcity, training subsets of sizes $N \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$ were sampled with exact 50/50 class stratification across 15 random seeds (seeds 0 to 14).

8.3. Data Preprocessing & Data Leakage Prevention

To prevent data leakage, Principal Component Analysis was fitted strictly on the training fold of each subsample. Min-max scaling parameters $\min(\mathbf{z}_{\text{train}})$ and $\max(\mathbf{z}_{\text{train}})$ were computed solely on training instances to normalize coordinates to $[0, \pi]$. Test instances were projected into the training-derived PCA coordinate frame and clipped to $[0, \pi]$.

10. Conclusion and Future Work
10.1. Key Achievements of the Study

This project successfully formulated, developed, and empirically benchmarked QKernel-Lite, providing a rigorous, Bonferroni-corrected comparison of Projected Quantum Kernels against matched classical transfer learning under extreme pediatric radiograph scarcity ($N \le 100$). The study identified the physical mechanism behind angle encoding collapse (phase wrapping turnaround), proved that scale compression restores monotonicity, demonstrated that multi-qubit entanglement degrades clinical separability, and refuted the hypothesis that discarded 2-qubit correlations caused the degradation.

10.2. Limitations of the Work

1. Simulation vs. Physical QPUs: All quantum circuits were simulated on classical state-vector engines (lightning.qubit) without physical device decoherence or readout noise.
2. Feature Space Ceiling: Pretrained ResNet-18 features saturated near 0.925 ROC-AUC, leaving limited margin for downstream non-linear kernel transformations.
3. 2D Planar Projection: The study utilized 28×28 grayscale radiographs; volumetric 3D CT/MRI scans with multi-slice correlations were not evaluated.

10.3. Permissible vs. Impermissible Bounded Scientific Claims
Permissible Claims: (1) On PneumoniaMNIST at $N \le 100$, M3-tuned is detectably inferior to classical RBF-SVM (M2) by $-0.030$ to $-0.124$ AUC ($p < 0.001$, survives Bonferroni); (2) The only circuit matching M2 within $\pm 0.02$ AUC is the 1-layer product state, which is a per-feature classical trigonometric map and trivially classically simulable; (3) Multi-qubit entanglement systematically degrades performance in this architecture.
Impermissible Claims: (1) Do not claim a general proof that quantum kernels cannot work on medical images; (2) Do not claim entanglement scrambles pathology universally; (3) Do not claim M3 beats or ties classical transfer learning.
10.4. Actionable Directions for Future Work

Future research directions include: (1) Deploying error-mitigated projected circuits on physical quantum processing units (e.g., IBM Quantum Heron or IonQ Forte); (2) Exploring group-theoretic geometric quantum embeddings preserving radiological symmetries; (3) Extending PQK formulations to high-dimensional volumetric 3D CT/MRI scans.

13.1. Appendix A: PennyLane Quantum Circuit & Projected Kernel Core Implementation
import pennylane as qml import numpy as np def make_pqk_qnode(n_qubits=8, n_layers=2, entangling=True): dev = qml.device("lightning.qubit", wires=n_qubits) @qml.qnode(dev, interface="autograd", diff_method="best") def circuit(x_scaled): # x_scaled is array of angles s * theta_i in [0, s*pi] for layer in range(n_layers): for i in range(n_qubits): qml.RY(x_scaled[i], wires=i) qml.RZ(x_scaled[i], wires=i) if entangling and n_qubits > 1: for i in range(n_qubits): qml.CNOT(wires=[i, (i + 1) % n_qubits]) # Measure local 1-qubit Pauli expectations: 3 * n_qubits observables obs = [] for i in range(n_qubits): obs.extend([qml.expval(qml.PauliX(i)), qml.expval(qml.PauliY(i)), qml.expval(qml.PauliZ(i))]) return obs return circuit def compute_projected_kernel_matrix(X_proj_train, X_proj_test, gamma): # Gaussian RBF evaluated over projected expectation vectors in R^(3n) diff = X_proj_train[:, np.newaxis, :] - X_proj_test[np.newaxis, :, :] dist_sq = np.sum(diff ** 2, axis=-1) return np.exp(-gamma * dist_sq)
