PROJECT ABSTRACT
For IEG313: Quantum Computing for Engineers at IIIT Kottayam, our team developed QKernel-Lite, an empirical benchmarking platform evaluating Projected Quantum Kernel Support Vector Machines (PQK-SVM) on pediatric chest radiographs (PneumoniaMNIST) under extreme clinical data scarcity ($N \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$). In scarce clinical regimes, deep convolutional networks fail due to severe parameter under-determination ($P \gg N$). While recent literature hypothesizes quantum advantage for small datasets, we conduct a fully paired 960-run empirical study ($n = 15$ seeds, df = 14) with strict 12-test Bonferroni family-wise error control ($\alpha = 0.00417$).

Our findings establish that standard angle encoding ($s = 1.00$) fails due to single-qubit phase wrapping turnaround ($U(\pi)^2|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ up to global phase). While CV-tuned scaling ($s \le 0.25$) suppresses wrapping and recovers competitive performance ($0.7717$–$0.8962$ AUC), M3-tuned remains statistically detectably below classical RBF-SVM (M2) across all sample sizes (deficits of $-0.1241$, $-0.0846$, $-0.0296$, and $-0.0288$ AUC; all survive Bonferroni). The only circuit family statistically equivalent to M2 within $\pm 0.02$ AUC ($N \ge 20$) is the 1-layer product state, which is a per-feature trigonometric map and trivially classically simulable. Multi-qubit entanglement systematically degrades performance, and adding nearest-neighbor ZZ correlators does not reverse this degradation.

Chapter 1: Introduction & Problem Statement
1.1. Clinical Scarcity & Parameter Under-Determination

In rare pediatric pulmonology and emergent pathology, clinical datasets frequently contain fewer than 100 expert-verified radiographs. Training deep convolutional networks from scratch under such sample scarcity ($N \le 100$) leads to catastrophic empirical failure. When model capacity ($P \approx 10^5$ to $10^7$ weights) vastly exceeds training instances ($P \gg N$), empirical risk minimization memorizes spurious background artifacts (e.g., patient orientation markers, edge shadows) rather than authentic radiological opacities. At $N = 10$, scratch CNNs overfit noise, yielding high variance and poor diagnostic generalizability.

1.2. The Quantum Kernel Inductive Bias Hypothesis

Recent quantum machine learning literature hypothesizes that encoding classical data into high-dimensional quantum Hilbert spaces can construct expressive non-linear representations. Specifically, Projected Quantum Kernels (PQK) (Huang et al., 2021; Alagiyawanna et al., arXiv:2601.03375) map classical coordinates into parameterized quantum states $\rho(\mathbf{x})$, then project the state back to classical metric spaces via expectation values of local Pauli observables. This projection was proposed to avoid the exponential concentration (barren plateaus) that destroys global fidelity kernels.

1.3. The Three Non-Negotiable Rules of the Scientific Control

A pervasive flaw in published literature is comparing quantum models against unregularized scratch CNNs, claiming "quantum advantage" over an easily beaten strawman baseline. To establish genuine scientific validity, a quantum kernel benchmark must satisfy three strict controls:

  1. Identical Feature Representations: The classical control (Model M2) and quantum model (Model M3) must consume the exact same preprocessed feature coordinates extracted from frozen ResNet-18 and compressed via PCA.
  2. Identical Downstream Optimizer: Both models must use the identical convex dual quadratic program ($C$-SVM) and the identical Gaussian RBF kernel formulation.
  3. Strict Seed & Budget Parity: All models must be evaluated across identical random training subsets with stratified class splits, with any post-hoc search degrees of freedom transparently accounted for.
The Scientific Control Guarantee: Because M2 and M3 share the exact same Gaussian formula $k(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \exp(-\gamma \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|^2)$ and the identical convex dual SVM solver, any observed performance delta isolates the non-linear quantum coordinate transformation $\mathbf{x} \mapsto \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ as the sole independent variable.

Chapter 2: Literature Review & Core Concepts
2.1. Fidelity Kernels vs. Exponential Concentration

Conventional quantum kernels compute global transition fidelity in Hilbert space:

$$k_{\text{fidelity}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = |\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}) | \psi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle|^2 = \text{Tr}\left(\rho(\mathbf{x})\rho(\mathbf{x}')\right)$$
(1)

As proved by Huang et al. (2021), fidelity kernels suffer from exponential concentration in $n$-qubit systems: as $n$ grows, $\langle \psi(\mathbf{x}) | \psi(\mathbf{x}') \rangle \to 0$ for almost all orthogonal states. The resulting Gram matrix rapidly approaches the identity matrix $K \approx \mathbf{I}$, destroying margin separability. What this means in practice is that the quantum state space becomes vast and barren: every patient scan appears completely orthogonal to every other patient scan, leaving the SVM classifier unable to discover decision boundaries.

2.2. Projected Quantum Kernel Formulation

To resolve concentration, Huang et al. proposed projecting the full $n$-qubit state $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ onto reduced 1-particle density matrices, evaluating expectations of local Pauli operators $P_k \in \{\sigma_x^{(i)}, \sigma_y^{(i)}, \sigma_z^{(i)}\}$:

$$E_k(\mathbf{x}) = \text{Tr}\left(\rho(\mathbf{x}) P_k\right), \quad k \in \{1, \dots, 3n\}$$
(2)

The projected quantum kernel evaluates a classical Gaussian RBF over these physical expectation vectors:

$$k_{\text{PQK}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \exp\left(-\gamma \sum_{k=1}^{3n} \left(E_k(\mathbf{x}) - E_k(\mathbf{x}')\right)^2\right)$$
(3)
2.3. The Foundational Alagiyawanna et al. Study

Alagiyawanna et al. (SLAAI-ICAI 2024; arXiv:2601.03375) applied projected quantum kernels to small-dataset image classification. While reporting 95% accuracy on MNIST and 90% on CIFAR-10, their study did not test real medical opacity data, used synthetic downsampling, and lacked matched classical RBF-SVM baselines. QKernel-Lite expands this line of inquiry into pediatric radiograph opacity benchmarks with rigorous statistical controls.

3.2. Detailed Pipeline Walkthrough

Stage 1 (Feature Extraction): Raw 28×28 radiographs pass through a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 backbone (11.7M weights frozen). Global average pooling outputs a 512-dimensional embedding capturing optical textures and density gradients without overfitting small samples.
Stage 2 (Dimensionality Reduction): Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is fitted strictly on training folds (preventing data leakage) to compress 512 features to $n = 8$ components (explaining ~75% cumulative variance), scaled to rotation angles $\theta_i \in [0, \pi]$.
Stage 3 (Quantum Encoding & Measurement): Implemented in PennyLane (lightning.qubit). For $L$ layers, each qubit undergoes rotations $R_Z(s\theta_i) R_Y(s\theta_i)$ interleaved with circular CNOT entangling gates. 24 local Pauli expectation values $\langle X_i \rangle, \langle Y_i \rangle, \langle Z_i \rangle$ are measured.

Engineering Significance: By caching the ResNet-18 embeddings and quantum expectations to disk, the quadratic kernel Gram matrix is evaluated in milliseconds, making the entire pipeline 100% deterministic and offline reproducible.

Chapter 5: Physical Diagnostics & Metric Space
5.1. The Phase Wrapping Mechanism

Under default angle scaling ($s = 1.00$), 2-layer rotations cause single-qubit expectations to undergo frequency doubling:

Fig 4. Phase wrapping mechanism. Unscaled 2L reaches $\langle X \rangle = -1$ at $\theta = \pi/2$ and wraps back to $\langle Z \rangle = +1$ at $\theta = \pi$ ($U(\pi)^2|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ up to global phase). Tuning to $s = 0.25$ restricts angles to $[0, \pi/4]$, restoring monotonicity.
5.2. Metric Concentration & Rank Scrambling
Fig 5. Physical diagnostics. (a) Variance collapse under entanglement. (b) Relative distance spread (classical drops roughly as $1/\sqrt{d}$). (c) Spearman rank correlation collapses toward 0 under entanglement, scrambling anatomical metric ordering.
Tested Negative Result: The ZZ Correlator Hypothesis:
To test if discarding correlations caused the deficit, we evaluated 8 nearest-neighbor two-qubit correlators $\langle Z_i Z_{i+1} \rangle$ on 8Q 2L Ent across 5 seeds. At $s = 0.25$, adding ZZ correlators decreased test AUC: $N = 10$ dropped $0.771 \to 0.758$ ($-0.013$); $N = 20$ dropped $0.835 \to 0.801$ ($-0.034$); $N = 50$ dropped $0.887 \to 0.877$ ($-0.010$); $N = 100$ dropped $0.892 \to 0.878$ ($-0.014$). Discarding correlations is not the root cause.

Chapters 7 & 8: Disclosures, Defense & References
7.1. Methodological Disclosures

1. Post-Hoc Scale Optimization: The scaling grid $s \in \{0.0625, \dots, 1.0\}$ was added after observing default $s = 1.0$ failure.
2. Tuning Budget Asymmetry: M3 explored 270 configs per fold vs. 36–54 for M2. Despite $5\times$ more tuning, M3 remained below M2.
3. Framework Migration: Ported reference synthetic TensorFlow pipelines to PyTorch and PennyLane for medical imaging.
4. Simulated Scarcity: PneumoniaMNIST was subsampled to $N \le 100$ while preserving a fixed 624-image test set.

8.1. Permissible vs. Impermissible Claims

Permissible: M3-default collapses; M3-tuned recovers but stays detectably below M2 across all $N$; only 1L unentangled product states match M2 (trivially classically simulable); entanglement systematically degrades performance; benchmark saturates near 0.93 AUC.
Impermissible: Do not claim general quantum kernel inferiority; do not claim entanglement scrambles pathology; do not claim M3 ties M2.
